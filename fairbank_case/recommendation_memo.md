### MEMORANDUM TO: Board of Directors, FairBank
### FROM: Santiago Angel Esteban Leyva Samanamud, Office of the CEO
### DATE: June 23rd, 2026
### RE: Algorithmic Bias in Small-Loan Approval – Assessment and Recommended Action Plan 
Our internal analysis has verified that, despite the model’s inherent racial blindness, FairBank’s automated small-loan algorithm generates fundamentally unfair results. White applicants are only rejected 18% of the time, compared to 30% of Black applicants who would have returned their loans. This disparity exposes the bank to significant reputational, regulatory, and ethical risk. A single approval threshold applied uniformly across demographically unequal populations, concentrating error among the group with lower average scores; historical and socioeconomic disparities that result in lower average loan-worthiness scores for Black applicants; and the possibility that prior discriminatory patterns are encoded in training data and proxy variables, even in the absence of explicit protected attributes, are the three potential sources of structural bias rather than model error (just 16%). The following steps are advised to resolve these problems and enhance the model’s fairness. To confirm our internal results and meet new fair-lending examination requirements, commission an impartial third-party algorithmic audit right away. The regulatory and legal ramifications of any threshold revisions should then be discussed with General Counsel Jeffrey Myers as soon as he returns from his business travel. Investigate equalized opportunity interventions in the near future, such as group-differentiated or context-aware thresholds and manual review pipelines for cases that are on the verge of approval. These could enable seasoned loan officers to consider qualitative aspects that the algorithm is unable to capture. Incorporate alternative credit signals that may more effectively reflect creditworthiness into the training data over the medium term and evaluate current proxy variables for possible disparate impact. In order for the board to hold management responsible for quantifiable progress toward equitable outcomes, it is advised that the targeted marketing program to underserved communities be expedited to gradually expand and rebalance the applicant pool while establishing algorithmic fairness monitoring (tracking demographic and opportunity parity on a quarterly basis).
